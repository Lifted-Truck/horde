If that self-divergence matches the C++-vs-JS rms and GROWS over the render (positive Lyapunov exponent), sample-exact parity is impossible in principle — Math.sin and std::sin are not identically rounded, and the dynamics amplify the last bit. [L0016] CALIBRATE THE DETECTOR BEFORE TRUSTING IT — run any new defect metric on a known-CLEAN and a known-BAD signal first; a metric that does not report ~zero on the clean case is measuring the signal, not the defect | canonical | added: 2026-07-28 | tags: parity-oracle, realtime-perf | lesson: THREE TIMES IN ONE SESSION I built a measurement instrument, got numbers that looked reasonable, and was measuring the wrong thing entirely. (1) An aliasing metric summed every non-harmonic FFT bin — it was accumulating Hann-window leakage from a dozen strong harmonics across thousands of bins, and reported a textbook-clean polyBLEP sawtooth at -27.7 dB instead of -149.5 dB. (2) A formant locator detrended the spectrum by +20log10(k) to cancel the saw's rolloff — that tilt over-weights the top octaves so hard it finds noise, and it reported 3190 Hz for an 800 Hz target. (3) A click detector thresholded |x[n]-x[n-1]| — on a 1200 Hz sine that is just the slope (~0.17/sample at 44.1 kHz), so it reported 2700 "jumps" in a perfectly clean signal, and I nearly chased a bug that did not exist. THE COMMON SHAPE: a threshold on the WRONG DERIVATIVE (or the wrong domain) measures the signal itself rather than the defect. WHY IT SURVIVES SCRUTINY: in all three cases the RANKING between configurations stayed roughly correct, so A/B comparisons still "worked" and nothing looked broken; only the absolute value was absurd. What saved me each time was noticing the absolute number was implausible against a reference I happened to know (a clean polyBLEP saw is ~-150 dB, not -27). That is luck, and it fails silently whenever I do NOT already know the right answer. THE RULE, which is cheap because both cases almost always already exist: before trusting a new detector, run it on (a) a signal known to be CLEAN — it MUST report ~zero, and if it does not, it is measuring the signal; and (b) a signal known to be DEFECTIVE — it MUST report large. Only then interpret real results. Corollary: plausible rankings are NOT validation. Domain-general — applies to any residue, noise floor, crosstalk, error rate, or A-minus-B metric where B is orders of magnitude larger than A. THE SPECTRAL CASE, absorbed here: when measuring a small quantity beside a large one, never define the small one as "total minus large" — find a place in the domain where the large quantity is structurally ABSENT and measure there. Summing every non-harmonic FFT bin measures Hann-window LEAKAGE from a dozen strong harmonics across thousands of bins, not aliasing; sampling at inter-harmonic midpoints ((k+0.5)*f0), where the nearest harmonic is hundreds of bins away, measures the residue. Applies to spectral residue, noise floors, crosstalk, and any A-minus-B metric where B is orders of magnitude bigger. | evidence: 2026-07-28 shape-lab session, all three instances measured and corrected: aliasing -27.7 -> -149.5 dB after inter-harmonic midpoint sampling (and it then revealed an 87 dB spread the broken metric had compressed to 15 dB, incl. the fractional-sync hot spot it hid entirely); formant locator replaced with a 1/3-octave envelope peak; click detector replaced with 2nd-difference outliers vs the robust local median, which then correctly read 0/s on integer sync and 300/s on ratio 4.37. | falsifier: a detector that passes the clean/defective calibration pair is later shown to be measuring the wrong quantity anyway — then calibration is insufficient and a stronger validation (e.g. an independently-derived reference implementation) is required. | absorbs: L0014 — the spectral case; consolidated 2026-08-11

[L0019] Humanised timing is CORRECTED error, not independent jitter | tier: canonical | added: 2026-07-29 | tags: timing, perception, humanize, midi, ensemble, doctrine, cross-project | lesson: Listeners judge ensemble togetherness from the SERIAL STRUCTURE of onset asynchrony, not from its variance. Independent per-note random offsets — what essentially every "humanize", "groove", or "feel" control does — produce the right variance with the WRONG autocorrelation, and read as sloppy rather than human. Model mutual error correction instead: each part nudges its offset toward the group's mean offset and then adds fresh motor noise, `off_i <- off_i - alpha*(off_i - mean_off) + noise_i`. The human-like lag-1 autocorrelation is then EMERGENT, not dialled in.
